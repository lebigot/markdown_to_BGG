# One-sentence overview

A **cooperative** game set in the **Cthulhu Mythos** universe, with a **rich**, **streamlined**, **thematic** and **almost narrative** experience, that justifies its **relatively long playing time** (2–4 hours).

# Learn more about the game

If you don't know anything about the game, I would suggest that you first check out this **short (10 minute) official teaser**:

(https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6PxySn6RCXw)

# Review

The **most positive points come first**, and the **worst ones** are **at the end** (according to my own tastes, but you can **judge each point based on your own tastes**).

## Pros

Very **thematic**:
- In-game **effects mirror their theme very well**. You thus get weapons, spells, get wounded, etc., with **effects that make sense thematically**. You also **cannot** typically go **much beyond the natural abilities** of characters (this is more realistic). You also have a card where a **competing group of adventurers** seem to find all these precious relics before you, à la Indiana Jones. Etc.
- Each turn, you are provided with **relatively detailed story bits**, a few lines long, that make picturing what is happening in game quite easy. These mini-stories even regularly seem to **magically make sense** in the context of what just happened (like when this merchant asks you if they are all saved… just after you dealt the final blow to Cthulhu's daughter!).
- You can get **clever double-sided cards**, with a **fixed front**—a Condition (like an Injury) or a Spell—, but a **variable back**, that you **cannot look at** before it resolves. This creates a **realistic uncertain future**, as you do not know exactly what will happen with this Leg Injury.

**Simple** and **streamlined** (it is easy to get back to the game). An example is that **you can buy objects** and services, but **the game eschews using money** for this (instead you test your Influence, which can also be thought of as money, but can also be used for other things). Combat is also simple, and **banding** against a Monster **requires no special rules**. Also, **Investigators** have **only a couple of abilities**, which makes them quite easy to use. Another example is that the **limit for _all_ the tokens** that you can only have a maximum number of is _two_ (Travel Tickets, Focus tokens, Improvements…). Etc.

**Epic**: you travel **all over the world**, and many, **many different events** can happen to you. This is **only magnified by the expansions**.

**Rich**. Many **thematically different things happen** to the Investigators (Encounters, events during the Mythos phase…). The **Investigators** are really **varied**.

**Ergonomic**. For example, cards that provide you with an action have the keyword "Action" in **bold**, so that it is easily spotted. Similarly, card abilities that modify your Skills have **icons that are easily spotted** (solid black shape). Etc.

**Easy to expand**: the expansions can **all be mixed together**, and they **don't introduce many rules**.

**Works well with 2–4 players**, according to the BGG player number poll. **Great for solo** play too, in my opinion, but you may decide that **playing solo with more than 1 Investigator** suits you best.

## Neutral

**Long setup** (10–25 minutes if you're _alone_ and _don't have any special insert_). You need to **fish** for some starting cards. You need to **build** the **deck** of events (Mythos cards). **Not many tokens** need to be put on the board, though. I don't mind the setup, but you may.

The **story bits** that you are provided with are **specific** enough that they **may sometimes feel repetitive**. The **number of combinations** of story bits and in-game situations is however **immense**, so no two games will tell the same overall story. **Expansions** provide a lot of contents, which helps with the variety of stories. It also helps to **add some contextual and thematic story bits yourself** to what is described on the cards, so as to create **new stories** around the various story bits from the game.

## Cons

The game comes with **only 4 dice**, but rolling 6 dice is relatively common.

I wish that some **abilities** (most notably Investigator abilities) had a **short thematic description** (like they do in (https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/188920/war-mine-board-game)). It is **not too hard to picture** what these abilities mean in real life, though.

The game is more about accomplishing **successive goals that are easy to describe** (like closing Gates) using **simple and _fixed_ tools** (like moving to a location that tends to give you Spells during Encounters) that give you **random outcomes** (mostly within a category, like getting more Influence)—the open Reserve of Assets is an exception, as it varies from game to game, but it remain small and the effects of Assets tend to be relatively limited, or to be simple variations on a theme (like weapons of different strengths). This can be **contrasted with** games that present **many varied tools upfront**, that change from game to game and that players pick—like the possible inventions that can be built in (https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/121921/robinson-crusoe-adventures-cursed-island), or the market in (https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/181530/runebound-third-edition). Also, players will **define priorities** in order to **manage pressure** more than they will navigate a richer, evolving scenario with an overarching theme (like, I would say, (https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/181530/runebound-third-edition) does in some scenarios, by having an AI move enemies around in some interesting way). The **resulting poorer agency** is however in my eyes **compensated by** the **rich stories** provided and created by the game.

# Advice

Ride the **thematic story** wave: **add you own fluff around the story bits** provided by the game, so as to create a **larger story** that you will **remember better** just because you spent time thinking about it.

Use the **[Focus action](https://eldritchhorrorgame.fandom.com/wiki/Focus)** and possibly the **[Gather resources action](https://eldritchhorrorgame.fandom.com/wiki/Gather_Resources)** introduced in expansions (they can be implemented with only the base game), as they will probably make you **feel better than doing nothing** on certain turns (e.g. when you are rested and ready to fight Monsters at your location).

**Add expansions**. **Forgotten Lore** gives **more replayability** to the core game and is considered **a must**. You have **4 small box** expansions, and **4 big box** ones (3 of them have an additional sideboard which is used in some cases), and the consensus is that you can mostly choose the ones you want **based on your tastes**.

**If you like the game**, seriously consider **grabbing expansions quickly**, as they are quite easy to integrate and leave in the game, and the editor published in **2018** what is officially **the last expansion**!
